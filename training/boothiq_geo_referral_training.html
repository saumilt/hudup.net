1. Radius Search

What it is. You can now search for doctors near a location. Type an address, a city, or a ZIP code, choose a radius (how far out to look), and BoothIQ returns every matching physician in all the nearby cities — not just the one you typed.

Why it helps. Reps plan trips and territories by geography. Instead of guessing which towns are "close," you get the exact list of cities inside your radius and all the doctors in them, ranked by distance.

How to run a radius search

  1. Open Medicare Search from the left sidebar.
  2. Find the highlighted "Near — address, city, or ZIP" box (the row with the map‑pin icon).
  3. Type any of these: a ZIP (06510), a city + state (New Haven, CT), or a full street address.
  4. Pick a radius: tap a quick chip — 10mi 25mi 50mi 100mi — or type your own number.
  5. Add any other filters you like (specialty, 340B color, RPM tier) and click Search.

After searching, a banner appears: "Showing doctors within 25 mi of New Haven, CT 06510 — 59 nearby cities." That confirms exactly what you're looking at.

TipRadius search also works on the Leads list. Use the "Near" filter there to pull up every existing lead around a city you're about to visit.
Good to knowOur Medicare data is organized by city, so a radius search returns whole cities whose center falls inside your circle. It's built to be accurate for trip planning, not GPS‑exact to a single street.

2. Map View

What it is. Any radius search can be viewed as an interactive map instead of a list. You'll see exactly where your prospects cluster.

How to open it

  1. Run a radius search (Section 1).
  2. In the results banner, use the toggle on the right: List Map.
  3. Click Map — the coverage map loads.

How to read the map

  • Red pin — your origin (the address/city/ZIP you searched from). Its label shows the location name.
  • Shaded circle — the radius you chose. Everything inside it is in range.
  • Teal dots — cities with matching doctors. Bigger dots = more doctors in that city.
  • Hover a dot to see the city and its doctor count; click it for a small popup with details.
  • Use + / − (top‑left) to zoom.
TipSwitch back to List anytime to work the results — add leads, open records, or page through the table.

3. Drive‑Time Radius

What it is. Sometimes "30 minutes away" is more useful than "20 miles away." The radius control has a Drive time mode that lets you search by minutes of driving.

How to use it

  1. In the radius controls, switch the mode toggle from Miles to Drive time.
  2. Enter the minutes — or tap a chip: 15m 30m 45m 60m.
  3. Optionally adjust the Avg mph (default is 45 mph) to match highway vs. city driving.
  4. Click Search. The banner shows the equivalent distance, e.g. "~23 mi (≈30 min @ 45 mph)."
How the estimate worksDrive time is a smart approximation: BoothIQ converts your minutes into miles using the average speed you set (minutes × mph ÷ 60). It does not use live traffic or turn‑by‑turn routing — it's a fast, dependable estimate for planning. Lower the mph for dense urban areas; raise it for open highways.
TipYour chosen mode (miles or drive‑time) is remembered when you save a view or a referral generator — reload it later and the exact radius comes back.

4. Referral Search

What it is. A dedicated page for referral partnerships. Start from a referring doctor's practice and find nearby doctors who could become referral partners — then turn them into leads in one click.

How to run a referral search

  1. Open Referral Search from the left sidebar.
  2. Referral generator (optional): name the referring source, e.g. "Dr. Jane Smith — Cardiology."
  3. Their practice: enter the referring doctor's address, city, or ZIP.
  4. Choose a radius (miles or drive‑time) and any filters — Specialty, 340B color, RPM tier.
  5. Click Find referral partners.

Turning a result into a lead

  • Each row has an Add lead button. Click it to create a CRM lead for that doctor.
  • The new lead is automatically tagged with its source ("Referral Search") and linked to the referral generator you named — so credit is tracked (see Section 5).
  • A row already in your CRM shows an "In CRM" badge instead, so you never create duplicates.
TipSwitch to Map view here too, to see partner density around the referring practice.

5. Saved Generators & the Referral Network

What it is. Save any referral source once, reload it instantly later, and track how many leads it has produced for you over time.

Save a referral generator

  1. Fill in the referral form (name, practice location, radius, filters).
  2. Click Save generator (top‑right). Give it a clear name if prompted.
  3. It now appears as a chip in the Saved generators bar at the top of the page.

Reload & track produced leads

  • Click a saved chip to instantly reload its area + filters and re‑run the search.
  • When leads have been created from that generator, the chip shows a "X leads" badge (e.g. 1 leads).
  • Click the badge to open a list of exactly which leads that referral source produced — click any one to jump to its record.
  • Remove a generator with the trash icon on its chip.
Why this mattersThis is your referral network scorecard. At a glance you can see which referring doctors actually send you business, so you know where to invest your relationship‑building time.

6. Tips, FAQ & Quick Reference

Frequently asked questions

QuestionAnswer
What can I type in the "Near / practice" box?A 5‑digit ZIP (06510), a city + state (New Haven, CT), or a full street address. BoothIQ figures out which one you entered.
Why do results show nearby towns I didn't type?That's the point of radius search — it returns every city whose center is inside your circle, ranked by distance.
Is drive‑time exact?No — it's a fast estimate (minutes × average mph). Adjust the mph for city vs. highway driving.
Do I need to install maps or get a key?No. The map and all location features are built in and free to use.
Will "Add lead" create duplicates?No. Doctors already in your CRM show an "In CRM" badge instead of an Add button.
How is referral credit tracked?Leads created from Referral Search are linked to the generator you named; the "X leads" badge counts them automatically.
